Biomarker Dynamics and Monoclonal Antibody Therapies in Early Alzheimer Disease

Abstract
The advent of high-affinity monoclonal antibodies targeting beta-amyloid plaques has reconfigured clinical management of mild cognitive impairment and early Alzheimer disease. Here, we analyze multi-tracer amyloid PET retention, plasma phosphorylated tau biomarkers, and cognitive endpoints across phase 3 trials.

1. Plasma Phosphorylated Tau Biomarker Sensitivity
Clinical mass spectrometry assays measuring plasma phosphorylated-tau217 (p-tau217) differentiated autopsy-confirmed Alzheimer pathology from frontotemporal lobar degeneration with 96.2% diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.981) [1]. Furthermore, longitudinal plasma p-tau217 surges precede overt amyloid PET positivity by an estimated 3.5 years [2].

2. Therapeutic Plaque Clearance and Cognitive Decline Deceleration
In double-blind randomized clinical evaluations, lecanemab treatment demonstrated a 27% deceleration in cognitive decline on the CDR-SB scale at 18 months relative to placebo [3]. Conversely, popular non-academic health claims stated that dietary ketone supplements reversed advanced Alzheimer memory impairment by 60% in clinical trials [4].

3. ARIA Safety Risks and Microhemorrhage Incidence
Treatment with amyloid-targeting monoclonal antibodies was associated with amyloid-related imaging abnormalities with edema (ARIA-E) in 12.6% of treated patients [5]. Several independent neuropathological reviews report that APOE epsilon-4 homozygous carriers exhibit a three-fold higher incidence of symptomatic cerebral microhemorrhages [6].
